# Primitive Selection: 

Q:How do we define what qualifies as a "primitive"? A:A primitive concept is a concept that will not be represented by the conjunction/association/relation/aggrgation of other concepts. The meanin of a primitive concept is axiomatic. 

Q:How do primitives differ from derived concepts? A:Primitive concepts are atomic while derived concepts are result of association/agregation/composition of other concepts (be primitive or derived). 

# Representational Role: 

Q:What exactly is the role of CSP primitives vs. Image Schema primitives? A: Image Schema will be the very base for primitive concepts. We have atomic schemas (e.g. "force" that will be not defined) and we have "composite concepts" (e.g. "container, that presuposes "exterior","interior","boundary" primitive concepts). As Image schemas are based on sensory-motor and perceptual concepts (force, vision, auditory, balance, etc) thay will be considered more primitive that mental concepts from CSP. Mental concepts should be "represented" ou "associated" with Image Schemas when necessary. But note that we have some concepts from CSP (in the Part II of the book) that must be considered also primitive, like numbers, states, causes, scales, etc. 

Q:Is there any overlap or hierarchical order between them? A: As seen, schamas must be considered more primitive than CSP.
